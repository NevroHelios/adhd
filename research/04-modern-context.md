# Are adult ADHD screeners outdated for modern work and technology? (2026-10-17)

Subagent report. Main session re-checked via Crossref: Kushlev 2016, Zhang & Markon 2021, Sibley 2018, Lewczuk 2024, Ustun 2017, Yeung 2022, Fuermaier & Niesten 2025, Alarachi 2024 (all resolve, titles match). Note: the "2025 APSARD guideline" came from the main session's prompt, not from the user.

**Premise correction:** the DSM-5 (2013) already modernized the adult examples ("mobile telephones" in A1g; "returning calls, paying bills, keeping appointments" in A1i; "preparing reports, completing forms" in A1f). The ASRS-5 (2017) is DSM-5-based. "Pre-smartphone" applies to the ASRS v1.1 (2005) and the WURS (1993). DSM-5-TR did not change the criteria (doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2022.1064141).

## 1. DIF / invariance
- ASRS-5: 6 items (4 DSM + 2 non-DSM), cutoff ≥14; general population sens 91.4 / spec 96.0 / AUC .94 / PPV 67.3% at 8.2% prevalence; clinical spec 74 / AUC .83. Ustun 2017 doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0298.
- ASRS-6: all items age-noninvariant, some gender-noninvariant; not unidimensional (disorganization / inattention / hyperactivity). Zhang & Markon 2021 doi:10.1177/1087054718808059.
- ASRS-18: age DIF on 5/18; older adults under-endorse motor hyperactivity (preprint). doi:10.64898/2026.04.06.26350233.
- ASRS-5 Danish Rasch: gender DIF on items 3, 4, 6. doi:10.1177/10870547241312575.
- 42 countries, N=72,627: partial invariance; 21.4% of women and 18.1% of men screen positive; low specificity. Lewczuk 2024 doi:10.1177/10870547231215518.
- Workers bifactor: ωH=.724, linked to burnout. Rosario-Hernández 2025 doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1702403.
- Anxiety contamination: "difficulty relaxing", "driven by a motor" load on anxiety. Alarachi 2024 doi:10.1177/10731911231225190.
- **No occupation/education DIF study exists for adult ADHD self-report.**
- Design: age/gender norms; internalized restlessness wording; collect occupation data for your own DIF analysis.

## 2. Digital environment
- Ra 2018 JAMA doi:10.1001/jama.2018.8931: OR 1.10 per high-frequency activity (adolescents, observational).
- **Kushlev 2016 CHI doi:10.1145/2858036.2858359**: within-subject; notifications on raised ADHD-like self-reports in non-clinical adults (some related effects didn't replicate, doi:10.1080/15213269.2024.2334025).
- Ding 2025 meta-analysis doi:10.1016/j.jpsychires.2025.07.009: problematic social media r=.361 with ADHD symptoms; larger post-COVID and with the ASRS.
- Thorell 2024 doi:10.1007/s00787-022-02130-3: reciprocal effects.
- **Untested**: whether heavy tech users without ADHD score falsely high on the ASRS.
- Design: low-tech context items; onset before smartphones / age 12; "does it improve with notifications off?"; treat phone items as context, not evidence.

## 3. Work / occupation
- Kessler 2005 JOEM doi:10.1097/01.jom.0000166863.33541.39: lost days/year blue collar 55.8, service 32.6, technical 19.8, professional 12.2. **Occupation changes impairment.**
- WFIRS-S work domain with N/A items = adaptable.
- Remote work: Ishimoto 2023 doi:10.2147/ndt.s414862 (industry-affiliated); Brooks 2024 Occup Med (doi inferred).
- Developers: Liebel 2024 doi:10.1145/3639475.3640107; ICSE 2025 doi:10.1109/ICSE55347.2025.00242 (1.8-4.4× more struggles, time management).
- Entrepreneurs: Wiklund 2016 doi:10.1016/j.jbvi.2016.07.001; Yu 2021 doi:10.1177/1042258719892987 (hyperactivity/impulsivity linked to better firm performance).
- Medical students: prevalence 1.7-38.9% depending on instrument (doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1684727).
- Drivers: Vaa 2014 RR 1.23; Aduen 2018 IRR 1.46.
- **Evidence = occupation-specific impairment, NOT occupation-specific symptoms.** Use modular N/A impairment questions.

## 4. Generative AI
- Thin: Pinto 2025 OzCHI doi:10.1145/3764687.3764713 (n=13); Campbell 2024 doi:10.1007/978-3-031-61688-4_10; Carik 2025 doi:10.1145/3701194; CHI 2025 doi:10.1145/3706598.3713670; Pimenova 2026 arXiv (AI as body doubling).
- **Fuermaier & Niesten 2025 doi:10.1007/s12207-025-09538-7: ChatGPT helps students feign ADHD**, and they are harder to detect.
- No evidence that AI scaffolding lowers screener scores (masking is speculative).
- Design: exploratory, unscored "reliance on tools/AI, and what happens without them" item; consistency/infrequency checks.

## 5. Trends 2020-2026
- Stimulant starts rose, most in young adults and women: Chai 2024 JAMA Psychiatry doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2023.5045.
- Telehealth share of stimulant Rx 1.4% (2019) → 38.1% (2022) (JAMA Netw Open 2024; citation not captured).
- TikTok: 52/100 top ADHD videos misleading. Yeung 2022 doi:10.1177/07067437221082854.
- FDA Adderall shortage since 2022-10-12, reportedly still listed 2026; methylphenidate since July 2023.
- Done Global founders convicted 2025-11-18; Ruthia He sentenced 2026-07-07 to 6 years (trade press). Cerebral non-prosecution agreement 2024-11-04. DEA telemedicine flexibilities through 2026-12-31.
- UK NHS ADHD Taskforce 2025: 8+ year waits in some areas. NICE NG87 essentially 2018.
- **APSARD adult guideline not yet published** (final review Aug 2026).

## 6. Confounders and PPV
- ASRS in major depression: PPV 21.4% (Dunlop 2018 doi:10.3390/bs8040037). In SUD: PPV .26 / NPV .97 (van de Glind 2013 doi:10.1016/j.drugalcdep.2013.04.010). Bipolar: ~1/3 false positives. German outpatients: 34.4% positive.
- **Sibley 2018 AJP doi:10.1176/appi.ajp.2017.17030298: ~95% of apparent late-onset ADHD excluded on full assessment** (often substance use).
- Sleep: Brevik 2017 doi:10.1111/acps.12756; doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1528979. Burnout: doi:10.1007/s10389-026-02730-2 (no test of score inflation).
- Design: mandatory sleep / mood / anxiety / substance screens; onset <12; impairment in 2+ settings; LR / plain PPV reporting.

## 7. Under-recognized groups
- Women and older adults need higher latent severity to endorse items (Zhang & Markon), so they are under-detected with raw cutoffs.
- High IQ: Milioni 2017 doi:10.1177/1087054714554933 (small n).
- Non-Western: lower East Asian scores, partial invariance; sparse data.

## Candidate modern-context item areas
| Area | Strength |
|---|---|
| Confounder screens + onset + cross-setting impairment | **Strong** (biggest PPV lever) |
| Age-neutral internalized restlessness | Moderate |
| Hyperactivity items that discriminate from anxiety | Moderate |
| Prioritization/time estimation in self-directed knowledge work | Moderate |
| Notifications/task-switching with contrast condition | Moderate as confound, weak as marker |
| Async message/email backlog | Weak-moderate |
| Remote-work boundaries | Weak |
| Modular N/A work-impairment domain | Moderate (design principle) |
| Reliance on apps/AI scaffolding | Speculative; exploratory, unscored |
| Consistency/infrequency checks vs AI coaching | Moderate |

## Where the hypothesis is NOT supported
1. "Validated scales are pre-smartphone": partly false (DSM-5, ASRS-5).
2. Measured bias is by age and gender, not tech cohort; no tests of cohort/tech DIF.
3. Profession-specific symptoms: untested. Impairment differs, symptoms aren't shown to.
4. "AI masks ADHD": no evidence; AI-assisted feigning is documented.
5. Phone-centric items likely **reduce** specificity (Kushlev).
6. The bigger accuracy problem online is base rate and confounding (~20% screen positive online; PPV 21-26% in comorbid samples; 95% of late-onset positives false), not outdated wording.
